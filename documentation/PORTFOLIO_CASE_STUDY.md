*THE ORIGIN STORY*
## 1. Born from a Cat-Themed Hackathon

*THE SPARK*
### 1.1 #hackthekitty & Nabi
PAWSE originated during **#hackthekitty 2026**, a hackathon organized by Coding Kitty under the playful theme of **"World Cat Domination Day."** 

Rather than treating the theme as an excuse to slap decorative cat graphics onto a generic timer, our team asked a deeper product question:  
> *"Can the calming, low-pressure presence of a cat solve the psychological friction people experience with traditional productivity tools?"*

Inspired by our real-life cat **Nabi** (who also inspired the PAWSE logo), we set out to build an intentional desktop companion that turns timed work into a supportive ritual.

*POST-HACKATHON MOMENTUM*
### 1.2 From 48-Hour Prototype to Real Product
What started as a hackathon experiment quickly proved its value. Testers noted that having a quiet virtual pet at their desk reduced work stress and made timer transitions feel less abrupt. Based on that positive validation, we decided to pursue PAWSE beyond the hackathon and launch it as a standalone desktop app this year.

*THE PROBLEM*
## 2. Why Another Productivity App?

While standard Pomodoro timers aim to boost productivity, three common design patterns frequently create friction for users:

*CLOCK WATCHING*
### 2.1 Timer Anxiety
Constantly watching a bright digital clock count down seconds pulls attention away from the actual task. Instead of immersing themselves in work, users frequently glance at the numbers, creating perceived time pressure and low-grade anxiety that disrupts focus.

*ACOUSTIC STRESS*
### 2.2 Alarm Shock
Most timers rely on sudden, high-decibel buzzer alarms to announce the end of a session. These abrupt sounds trigger an acoustic startle reflex, spiking stress and jarring the user out of their thought process rather than providing a gentle transition.

*RIGID STRUCTURES*
### 2.3 One-Size-Fits-All Work Intervals
Traditional Pomodoro tools lock users into a single rigid cadence (usually 25 minutes of work / 5 minutes of break). In reality, different types of tasks demand different cognitive rhythms—quick administrative tasks benefit from short sprints, while programming or writing require longer periods of uninterrupted immersion.

*THE SOLUTION*
## 3. Companion-Driven Productivity

PAWSE pairs proven productivity principles with companion dynamics to directly solve each friction point:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                                                        │
│   FRICTION POINT                     PAWSE COMPANION SOLUTION                          │
│   ─────────────────────────────      ──────────────────────────────────────────────    │
│   1. Timer Anxiety (Clock Watching)  → Optional "Cat Mode": Hides digits, communicates │
│                                        state through ambient feline animation.         │
│   2. Alarm Shock (Harsh Buzzers)     → Acoustic Redesign: Calming continuous purr loop │
│                                        paired with gentle transition meows.            │
│   3. Rigid Intervals                 → 3 Companion Cadences: Fixed presets tailored   │
│                                        for Sprints, Classic Focus, or Deep Flow.       │
│                                                                                        │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

*TACKLING TIMER ANXIETY*
### 3.1 Low-Stimulation "Cat Mode"
PAWSE does not force users to abandon the traditional clock, but it provides a dedicated alternative: **Cat Mode**. 
* In this mode, the numeric countdown is completely hidden. 
* The user's focus/break state is communicated entirely through ambient feline animation—a sleeping cat breathing slowly while you work, waking up and stretching when break time arrives.
* This allows users to stay aware of their session rhythm in their peripheral vision without the constant pressure of a ticking clock.

*TACKLING ALARM SHOCK*
### 3.2 A Softer, Feline-Inspired Audio Landscape
To prevent jarring disruptions, the audio experience was redesigned around natural cat sounds:
* **During Work:** An optional, continuous low-frequency **purring loop** acts as a gentle white-noise anchor to support sustained concentration.
* **At Transitions:** Sudden harsh buzzers are replaced with a **soft, gentle meow chime** that alerts the user without startling them.

*TACKLING RIGID INTERVALS*
### 3.3 Three Curated Companion Cadences
For our MVP, we designed three fixed timer presets matched with distinct companion personalities, allowing users to choose the cadence that fits their current task:

| Companion | Presets (Work / Short / Long) | Focus Style | Personality & Use Case |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Ginger** *(Orange Cat)* | **15m / 3m / 12m** | **Rapid Sprints** | Energetic and playful; tailored for quick task bursts, email triage, or combating afternoon fatigue. |
| **Tux** *(Tuxedo Cat)* | **25m / 5m / 20m** | **Classic & Paced** | Methodical scholar; follows the standard Pomodoro rhythm for balanced daily work. |
| **Void** *(Black Cat)* | **50m / 10m / 40m** | **Deep Flow** | Calm and focused; structured for software development, technical writing, and immersive problem-solving. |

*BEHAVIORAL PSYCHOLOGY*
### 3.4 How Companions Foster Focus: Emotional Anchoring & Gentle Boundaries
A virtual companion helps productivity by introducing a calming desktop presence—functioning like digital "body doubling" where working alongside a quietly resting pet creates a grounded atmosphere:
* **Focus Time:** Cat interactions are intentionally disabled during active work intervals to eliminate fidgeting and distractions.
* **Break Time:** Once the break starts, **petting unlocks**. Clicking the companion triggers gentle purrs, cheerful meow sounds, and random cat trivia facts in a speech bubble, providing a lighthearted mental reset before the next work block.

*DESIGN PROCESS & TOOLKIT*
## 4. UI/UX Process & Design Systems

*DESIGN STACK*
### 4.1 Tools
* **Mobbin:** Benchmarked real-world desktop patterns, focusing on floating HUDs, minimalist menu bar apps, compact audio controls, and collapsible dashboard sidebars.
* **FigJam:** Mapped the session state machine, interval flows, and the behavioral transitions between work, break, and idle companion states.
* **Figma:** Built wireframes, rapid prototypes, responsive window layouts, interactive micro-animations, and a tokenized design system for production handoff.

---

*DESKTOP ERGONOMICS*
### 4.2 Dual Windows & Compact Alternates

> 🖼️ **Bento Grid Card 1: Main & Dashboard Windows**  
> PAWSE pairs an active companion timer with an independent settings and analytics dashboard. We separated them into two distinct windows to keep the main timer clean, lightweight, and distraction-free—giving users a dedicated space to review weekly progress and adjust audio preferences without cluttering their workspace or interrupting an active focus session.

> 🖼️ **Bento Grid Card 2: Compact Window Alternates**  
> To adapt to limited screen space, users can shrink the main timer into one of two compact views based on their working style: a minimal **timer-only bar** for quick, glanceable countdowns while multitasking, or an ambient **cat-only desk pet** that hides numbers completely to support users who experience stress or distraction from ticking clocks.

*INTERACTION DESIGN*
### 4.4 Break Gamification & Habit Reinforcement
To make session completion satisfying without becoming a distraction:
* **The 4-Fish Treat Milestone:** Every completed work interval earns a fish treat. Collecting all 4 fills the progress bar and unlocks the long restorative break.
* **Interactive Petting & Trivia:** During breaks, clicking the companion triggers an interactive petting state with sound feedback and an animated speech bubble displaying educational cat facts.

*FRONTEND IMPLEMENTATION*
## 5. Bringing Designs to Life: My Frontend Role

*FROM FIGMA TO CODE*
### 5.1 Translating UI to Framework-Free Vanilla Web Tech
As both the lead designer and frontend developer, I translated my Figma designs directly into code using lightweight, framework-free Vanilla HTML5, CSS3, and JavaScript inside Electron. This approach kept the desktop app fast and featherweight while preserving complete design fidelity—enabling me to implement native CSS design tokens for instant companion theming, engineer crash-resilient local state tracking so timer progress is never lost, and build custom weekly analytics charts without relying on bloated external libraries.

*RETROSPECTIVE*
## 6. Key Hackathon Takeaways

*EMPATHY-FIRST DESIGN*
### 6.1 What a System Hides Is as Important as What It Shows
Designing **Cat Mode** demonstrated that emotional design often comes from removing stress triggers. Providing a clock-free view solved the anxiety that causes many users to stop using timers.

*CREATIVE CONSTRAINTS*
### 6.2 Theme Constraints Spark Practical Innovation
The hackathon's "cat theme" encouraged us to explore sound and animation as functional UX cues rather than superficial decorations.

*HYBRID STRENGTHS*
### 6.3 The Value of the Designer-Developer Hybrid Role
Writing the frontend code directly from my own Figma designs eliminated handoff friction and ensured that animations, spacing, and desktop window behaviors matched the intended design vision.
